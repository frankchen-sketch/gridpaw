## 正文（发布时粘贴为 caption，图片按上图顺序插入）

I thought I'd breeze through a paint-by-numbers puzzle. Then a 15×15 happened and I stared at one row for ten solid minutes. Nonograms have humbled me before — but GridPaw's cat version is the one I keep coming back to.

[IMG-1]

If you've never tried one: row clues sit on the left, column clues on top, and each number is the run-length of a filled block — "2 2" means two blocks of 2, with at least one gap between. Match every row AND every column and a hidden picture appears. In GridPaw's case it's always a cat, and the reveal — the whole pixel grid flipping to amber in about a second — is honestly the best part.

Three things caught me off guard:

1. Tapping cycles blank → filled → cross → blank. Guess wrong and the game doesn't scold — just a red shake, an auto ✕, and on you go. It never locks your input.

[IMG-2]

2. The 💡 Hint reveals exactly one correct cell, and you get 3 per level, refilled fresh every level. I burned mine on Medium like a rookie. Save them for Hard.

3. The clue numbers are clickable — tap one and its whole row/column lights up for a couple seconds. Free focus, use it constantly.

[IMG-3]

There's a 4-part guided tutorial (tiny cat boards, impossible to get stuck), then an 11-level campaign: Easy 5×5 → Medium 10×10 → Hard 15×15. Beat all 11 and you unlock the Daily puzzle, which is date-seeded — everyone on the internet gets the exact same board that day, alternating medium and hard. Same-board bragging rights with strangers: real.

[IMG-4]

No account, no signup, plays great on a phone, and your progress saves itself in the browser. The win screen even shares itself Wordle-style, with the most honest caption I've seen: "zero wrong taps… mostly 😏"

[IMG-5]

The one I play on breaks: [gridpaw.com/nonogram/](https://gridpaw.com/nonogram/)
