# Shikaku for Beginners: How to Solve Your First Puzzle

Shikaku arrives with almost no fanfare. No tutorial army, no pop-up ads selling coins, nothing. Just a grid, some numbers, and one rule that fits in a sentence: every number is the area of the rectangle that must surround it. Cover the grid with rectangles, no overlaps, each number locked inside its own rectangle. Puzzle solved. It is one of the quiet classics from Nikoli, the Japanese house that gave the world Sudoku, and it runs on pure spatial logic. No math beyond multiplication, no luck, no pay-to-win.

GridPaw serves this puzzle free, wrapped in cat art and unlimited procedurally generated boards. No sign-up required, playable in two clicks. The one-page help guide at [gridpaw.com/help/shikaku/](https://gridpaw.com/help/shikaku/) is the reference version of everything below. But first, one piece of advice: start by hunting the 3s and the other prime-number cells. They are the fastest wins on any board, and once you see why, everything else clicks.

## The one rule is all you need

A 6 can become a 2×3 or a 3×2. A 4 can be a 2×2, a 1×4, or a 4×1. A number does not care about shape, only about area: width times height must equal the digit inside. When you drag across cells, the game checks that silently and refuses any rectangle that breaks the rule.

Beginners stall because big numbers look flexible and small numbers look boring. Flip that instinct. A 3 can only be a 1×3 or a 3×1. A 2 can only be a 1×2 or a 2×1. Prime numbers have exactly two ways to be drawn, and usually the board geometry kills one of them. A 3 in a corner can only run along the edge. A 5 squeezed between two others is often already solved for you. Hunt these cells first, and the rest of the board stops being a wall of options. This is the habit that carries you all the way to the biggest grids.

## Your first puzzle, three steps

**Step 1: Open the game.** Go to [gridpaw.com](https://gridpaw.com) and press play. Level 1 is a guided Tutorial on a 3×3 grid. It walks you through every rule step by step, and you cannot fail it. No account, no install, no credit card. The tutorial exists to teach you how dragging feels, so take it slowly.

**Step 2: Draw one rectangle.** Click a cell and drag to another cell. A translucent preview follows your cursor, so you can see exactly what you are about to commit. Release, and the rectangle locks in. If the number inside matches the area you covered, it stays. If not, the game simply refuses to place it: the rectangle either mismatches the number's area, overlaps an already-placed rectangle, or swallows more than one number. Adjust the drag and try again.

**Step 3: Cover the whole grid.** Keep drawing rectangles until the last empty cell is filled. The puzzle auto-solves the moment your final rectangle is correct. That instant, when a scattered grid snaps into a complete pattern, is the hook. It is small, quiet, and deeply satisfying.

Playing on a phone? Tap the first cell, then tap the opposite corner. No dragging needed.

## The toolbar is your friend

Two buttons matter when you are stuck. **Undo** removes the last rectangle and costs nothing. **Hint** lights up one not-yet-placed rectangle that is definitely correct, for three seconds, so you have time to study it. You get three hints per level, the badge shows the count, and the counter refills to three when you move on. My advice: refuse the hint until the endgame. When the board is almost done and only two lonely rectangles remain, that is when a hint earns its keep.

**New puzzle** discards the current board and generates a fresh one at the same difficulty. **Sound** toggles the audio. **Daily Challenge** serves the same puzzle to every player, with a larger Weekend Super board on Saturdays and Sundays.

## From Tutorial to Legend

Seven tiers stand between you and the hardest boards. Tutorial is a guided 3×3. Easy is 5×5, Medium is 5×6, Medium-Hard is 6×6, Hard is 7×8 and wears a flame badge, Expert sits in between, and Legend caps out at 8×8. Levels are sequential: finish one, hit Next, and you land on the next tier with a fresh puzzle. No grinding, no replaying the same layout forever.

<!-- MEDIUM IMAGE: help-shikaku-4.png (难度徽章特写，插在本段之后) -->

The habit that works at 3×3 keeps working at 8×8. Find the primes first. Then take the biggest numbers, because they have the fewest factor pairs. Then look at edges and corners, where geometry narrows the options. You are not solving forty rectangles at once. You are solving one forced rectangle, then another, then another, and the whole board follows.

## Winning is a popup with four buttons

When the grid is fully covered and every number sits in its own matching rectangle, the victory popup appears. You get four options. **Next** jumps to the next level and tops your hints back up to three. **Retry** replays the same level with a new layout. **Close** leaves you on the finished board, and **Share** hands you Copy, 𝕏, Reddit, and Save, so your win can go anywhere. At level 5, players without an account see one optional sign-up prompt. It has a "Maybe later" button, and you never have to use it. Playing without an account always works.

<!-- MEDIUM IMAGE: help-shikaku-5.png (胜利弹窗，插在本段之后) -->

Shikaku rewards the patient, and patience here has a trick: the first 3 you see is the whole board in miniature. Find it, force it along an edge, and watch the options collapse. The rest of the puzzle solves itself. Your first Tutorial run takes ninety seconds. Your first Legend board might take an evening. Both end the same way: a complete grid and a small, satisfying snap. Go find your first 3.
